Getting a flu shot every year is one of the simplest and most effective things you can do to protect your health — and your family's. The CDC recommends that everyone 6 months and older gets vaccinated annually, ideally before the end of October.

If you live in Lake County and are uninsured, underinsured, or simply looking for a free or low-cost option, you have real choices. This guide covers every verified location where Lake County residents can get a flu shot for free or at reduced cost in 2026.

Low-cost flu shots with insurance — or affordable without

If you have insurance, flu shots are covered at $0 cost under the Affordable Care Act at most pharmacies. If you don't have insurance, pharmacy flu shots typically cost $25–$45 out of pocket — more affordable than an urgent care visit if you get sick.

Free flu shots for children — Vaccines for Children (VFC) program

Children who are uninsured, Medicaid-eligible, or underinsured may qualify for free vaccines through the federal Vaccines for Children (VFC) program. All three Lake County Health Department clinic locations participate in VFC.

To check if your child qualifies, call the LCHD at (847) 377-8000 or visit the Lake County Health Department website.

WIC participants — free flu shot referrals

If you or a family member receives WIC (Women, Infants and Children) benefits, the Lake County WIC program provides free flu vaccine referrals as part of your regular wellness appointments. Contact the LCHD WIC office at (847) 377-8130 to ask about flu vaccination options at your next visit.

When should you get your flu shot?

The CDC recommends getting vaccinated by the end of October each year, before flu season peaks. However, getting vaccinated later — even in January, February, or beyond — still provides protection. Flu viruses circulate as late as May in Illinois.

The Lake County Health Department offers flu shots year-round by appointment, not just in fall, so there's no wrong time to call and schedule.

What to bring to your flu shot appointment

  • Insurance card — if you have one. Most sites do not require it.
  • ID — a driver's license, state ID, or school ID. Not always required at LCHD clinics.
  • Children's immunization records — if bringing a child, especially for VFC eligibility.
  • Wear a short-sleeved shirt — the shot goes in your upper arm.

At Lake County Health Department locations, no documentation is required for uninsured patients. Call ahead if you have concerns.
